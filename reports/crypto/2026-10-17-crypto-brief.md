## 5. US government and regulation

**Congress**
- **CLARITY Act (H.R. 3633):** It would split crypto oversight between the SEC and the CFTC. It is stalled.
  - The House passed it on July 17, 2025, by 294–134 (Triple-A).
  - The Senate Banking Committee advanced its version 15–9 on May 14, 2026 (Triple-A).
  - The Senate left for August recess on Aug 8 with no floor vote (ABA Banking Journal).
  - A Senate vote to move forward failed 49–50 in mid-September (DeFiRate). Law firm Orrick dates the failure to Sept 15.
  - Law firms now say federal agencies, states, and courts are filling the gap (Troutman Pepper Locke, Orrick). One commentator argues the bill is not dead (Finextra).
  - I found no new floor action in the last day.
- **Low-profile bills, amendments, and quiet rule changes in Congress:** Nothing found. I did not search congress.gov.

**SEC**
- **Custody proposal:** On Oct 1–2, the SEC proposed rules for how investment advisers hold crypto. Chair Paul Atkins said existing rules had not kept up with the market (CoinDesk, CNBC).
- **Quorum rule:** The SEC also changed its quorum rule. Two commissioners are now enough, down from three. If one of the two is conflicted out, the remaining commissioner can still act (CoinDesk). This got little attention.
- **Tokenized stocks:** OKX filed notice to trade tokenized stocks under the SEC's "Innovation Exemption." It would register as a Tokenized Securities Venue. The filing reportedly lists stocks from more than 60 US public companies (Lexology weekly blog, Oct 12).

**CFTC**
- **Federal framework proposal:** Early in October, the CFTC proposed a federal framework for crypto trading (The Hill, The Block, Troutman).
  - It would explain how existing commodities law applies to digital asset transactions.
  - It would create a new "crypto asset market" category of exchange registration.
  - It asks for public comment on preventing abusive practices.
  - It came after Congress failed to pass CLARITY.
  - Analysts at TD Cowen say the framework could face a 2028 election deadline (The Block, Oct 12).
  - I did not find the comment deadline.
- **Gemini:** A judge refused to reopen Gemini's $5M CFTC settlement. Gemini and the CFTC had both asked to cancel the penalty (crypto.news, about Oct 16).

**Treasury and bank regulators**
- **OCC debanking review (Oct 14):** The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency found that several large banks restricted services to crypto issuers, exchanges, and administrators. It blamed vague "reputational risk" policies and, in some cases, financial-crime concerns (Troutman). This is a bank regulator, not Treasury.
